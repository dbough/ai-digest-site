Tidal to ban fully AI-generated music on December 9, removing tens of millions of tracks

First reported 2026-11-24

Tidal will ban fully AI-generated music on December 9, reversing its earlier approach of labeling and demonetizing such tracks. Tidal's music lead said that 43 percent of tracks distributed to the platform over the past 90 days were fully AI-generated, and that the company expects to delete roughly 29 million AI-generated songs and reject 80,000 to 85,000 tracks per day. Tidal will still accept songs made with AI assistance.

Why it mattersA major streaming platform moving from labeling to outright bans sets a stronger precedent for how creative platforms handle generative content.
For buildersPlatforms enforcing AI-content rules will need reliable detection and clear distinctions between assisted and fully generated work, which is a real product requirement for content tools.

Pros

  • The ban responds to a concrete flood of low-quality AI uploads and aims to protect human artists.
  • The line between fully generated and AI-assisted work is at least stated clearly.

Cons

  • Enforcing a fully-generated versus assisted distinction depends on detection, which is hard to do accurately.
  • Deleting millions of tracks may harm some creators whose work is misclassified.

3/5 AGENTSRESEARCH

New benchmarks and evaluation studies show agents still fail at everyday tasks and can cause harm during recovery

First reported 2026-11-24 · Latest update 2026-11-25

TechCrunch reports on a new benchmark showing that even the most advanced AI models struggle to complete everyday tasks reliably and safely. An arXiv study measures harm from partial code executions in AppWorld agents, finding that recovery attempts after failure can alter external state. A related arXiv report, Taxi, describes a system for clustering and querying millions of agent steps at scale. Together they point to reliability and observability as the main limits on deploying agents.

Why it mattersAgent deployments depend on evaluation and debugging tools that catch harmful partial failures, which are harder to see than simple task failures.
For buildersWhen agents write code that issues multiple API writes, design idempotent operations and log state changes so that retries cannot silently cause damage.

Pros

  • Concrete failure measurements make it easier to design safer agent harnesses.
  • Scalable analysis tools could help teams find failure patterns in long agent runs.

Cons

  • Results are from benchmarks and recorded trajectories, not large production deployments.
  • The harm measurements rely on specific environments, so they may not transfer directly.

Coding agents move into team workflows, data platforms, and RL infrastructure

First reported 2026-11-25

Vercel launched an experimental Board where humans and coding agents claim leased tasks, declare dependencies, and sync with Slack, GitHub, and Linear. At QCon San Francisco, OpenAI's data infrastructure lead described using Codex agents for open-ended Spark upgrade work, arguing agents are most useful for operational tasks that are hard to turn into fixed playbooks. ByteDance Seed and HKU open-sourced Laminar, an asynchronous RL post-training framework for LLMs and long-horizon agents, tested on Qwen models across multi-node GPU clusters.

Why it mattersCoding agents are shifting from single-session assistance to coordinated work across tasks, teams, and large infrastructure migrations.
For buildersTask-claiming and dependency tracking for agents, plus asynchronous RL for long rollouts, are patterns worth copying for multi-agent coding setups.

Pros

  • Shared task boards make it easier to mix human and agent work without losing track of dependencies.
  • Asynchronous RL can cut GPU idle time when trajectories vary widely in length.

Cons

  • Coordination layers add operational complexity and new failure modes.
  • Laminar's results are reported by its authors and by a news summary, not independently benchmarked.
