How this works

AI Briefing is a daily summary of the most important developments in AI: new models, research, tools, business moves, and policy. It's written to be read in a few minutes, with links to every original source.

Please read. This briefing is selected and written automatically by AI, and no person reviews it before it's published. It can contain mistakes, miss nuance, or misstate what a source says. The creator of this site is not responsible for the accuracy of this information. Always read the linked original sources and use your own judgment before acting on anything here.

1. Finding the news

Every morning (issues are up by 7 a.m. Eastern) an automated process checks a fixed list of about thirty sources for anything published since the previous issue:

  • AI labs and research teams: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Google AI, Meta AI, Microsoft Research, Hugging Face, NVIDIA, AWS, and GitHub
  • Research papers: new arXiv papers in AI, machine learning, and language models
  • Technology journalism: The Verge, Wired, Ars Technica, TechCrunch, VentureBeat, MIT Technology Review, InfoQ, and others
  • Newsletters and independent writers: Simon Willison, Ethan Mollick's One Useful Thing, Import AI, Latent Space, TLDR AI, and The Rundown AI
  • Community and policy: highly upvoted Hacker News posts, NTEN, and Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology

That's usually a few hundred articles, many of them covering the same events.

2. Choosing and summarizing

All of those articles go to one AI model (Anthropic's Claude) together, in a single pass. The model:

  • Groups coverage of the same event into one story, so you don't read the same news five times.
  • Rates each story's importance from 1 to 5. Only stories rated 3 or higher make it into the issue.
  • Separately rates how relevant each story is to five kinds of work. A story that matters a lot to one of them, but isn't a top story, shows up under "Especially Relevant to Your Work":
    • Developer
    • Tech Consulting
    • Entrepreneur/Small Business
    • Nonprofit
    • Coaching & Human Impact
  • Writes a short summary with pros and cons, plus "Worth Watching" items and one topic worth exploring further, each linked to its sources.

The "first reported" and "latest update" dates on each story come from the sources' own publish dates, not from the AI. Images come from the linked articles.

3. No human in the loop

No person selects, writes, edits, or fact-checks the stories. Each issue is published automatically and isn't reviewed by a person first. That's why every story links to its original sources, and why you should read them and use your own judgment before acting on anything here.
